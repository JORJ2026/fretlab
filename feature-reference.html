Getting around

1. Welcome to FretLab [Shift+D starts this demo]

What: FretLab shows any scale, chord or interval on a fretboard, plays it, and teaches it – on guitar and 12 other instruments, fully offline.

How: Use Next / Back (or the ← → keys). Space pauses and resumes auto-play. Press Esc to leave: your own settings are put back exactly as they were.

When: Any time you want to see what the app can do, or want a refresher after an update.

2. The header menu [H]

What: The top bar with the mode tabs and the big buttons (Lessons, Games, Video, Library, Guide). It is collapsed to a slim bar by default to leave room for the neck.

How: Click ▾ Menu (or press H) to open it, ▴ Hide menu to close it. Your choice is remembered.

When: Open it when you want to switch mode or open a full-screen area; close it when you just want to practise.

3. Mode tabs [1–6]

What: Six ways to look at the neck: Scales, Chords, Intervals, Progressions, Explore (mark your own notes) and Quiz.

How: Click a tab, or press 1–6.

When: Scales for soloing, Chords for rhythm playing, Intervals for ear and theory, Progressions for songs, Explore for free marking, Quiz to test yourself.

4. Navigate menu [G]

What: A jump menu listing every section of the page.

How: Click ☰ Navigate (or press G) and pick a section; it opens and scrolls into view.

When: When the page is long and you want a section fast.

5. Simple view / Show all sections [O]

What: The Beta starts in a calm simple view with only the neck and its controls. Show all sections reveals every tool.

How: Click Show all sections to reveal everything, or ⊞ / ⊟ (or O) to expand or collapse all cards.

When: Use the simple view for practising, the full view when you explore the tools.

6. Tools menu

What: A menu of extra actions grouped in one place.

How: Click ⋯ Tools and choose an action.

When: When the header gets crowded, especially on a phone.

7. Quick search

What: Type any scale, chord or section name and jump straight to it.

How: Click 🔎, type a few letters, press Enter.

When: When you know the name of the scale or chord you want.

8. Undo and redo

What: Steps backwards and forwards through your recent changes to key, scale, chord and settings.

How: Click ↶ or ↷.

When: After changing something by mistake.

9. Theme [Shift+T]

What: Switches between light and dark. Seven more themes (sepia, midnight, forest, rose, stage red, high contrast and automatic) are in Settings.

How: Click 🌙 (Shift+T cycles every theme).

When: Dark for stages and evenings, light for daylight, high contrast for low vision.

10. Help and shortcuts [?]

What: A short list of every keyboard shortcut, with a search box.

How: Click ? or press ?.

When: When you want to learn the quick keys.

11. Focus mode [F]

What: Hides everything except the neck.

How: Press F (Esc leaves).

When: For recording, teaching or looking at the neck on a small screen.

12. Presentation mode [Shift+P]

What: A big, clean view for projectors and lessons.

How: Click 🖥 or press Shift+P.

When: When you are showing the neck to a class or audience.

13. Mute and volume [Shift+M]

What: Mute everything with one button; the 🔊 slider sets the master volume.

How: Click 🔈, or drag the slider.

When: To practise silently or to balance with a backing track.

14. Tip of the day

What: A small hint at the top. Next tip shows another; ✕ hides them for good.

How: Click Next tip.

When: While you are learning your way round.

Scales & controls

15. Choose a scale

What: 182 scales in families – major and its modes, minor types, pentatonics, blues, jazz, world and exotic scales.

How: Open the Scale menu and choose, or type in the search box. The neck updates at once.

When: Pick the scale that matches the key or mood of the music you are playing.

16. Search for a scale

What: A search box that filters the scale list as you type.

How: Type part of a name, for example “harm”, then press Enter.

When: When you do not know which family a scale lives in.

17. Root note [← →]

What: Twelve buttons choose the key (the root note).

How: Click a note, or use ← → to move the root one semitone (hold Shift for a fifth).

When: To play the same scale in another key.

18. Spelling

What: Chooses sharps, flats or automatic correct spelling for the key.

How: Click the Spelling button to cycle: Auto, Sharps ♯, Flats ♭.

When: Auto gives you proper note names (for example F♯ in G major, B♭ in F major).

19. Play the scale [Space]

What: Plays the whole scale up and down with the sound you chose in Settings.

How: Click ▶ Play scale or press Space. ■ stops it.

When: To hear a scale before you learn it, or to practise along.

20. Direction

What: Up, down, up and down, or random order when the scale plays.

How: Choose a Direction from the menu.

When: Use random for ear training.

21. Loop

What: Repeats the scale again and again.

How: Tick Loop and press Play; press Stop to end.

When: To play along as long as you like.

22. End on root chord

What: After the scale, plays the main chord that belongs to it, so the ear hears where the scale “lands”.

How: Tick End on root chord, then press Play.

When: To hear the tonic chord and tell a mode’s mood.

23. Random

What: Picks a random root and scale (or chord) to practise.

How: Click 🎲.

When: For warm-ups and surprise practice.

24. Save a favourite

What: Stores the current view (mode, key, scale, chord, tuning) in your Favourites.

How: Click ★ Save; open it again from the Favourites section.

When: To return to a setup you use often.

25. Scale library

What: A browsable library of all scales with note spellings and formulas.

How: Click 📚 Library.

When: To compare scales and read their note names.

26. Compare two scales

What: Shows a second scale on the same root on top of the first, so you see what is different.

How: Choose a scale in the Compare menu.

When: To understand how Dorian differs from natural minor, for example.

27. Overlay a chord

What: Draws a chord of the current key on top of the scale.

How: Choose a chord in the Overlay menu.

When: To see which scale notes make up the chord you are playing over.

The fretboard

28. The fretboard

What: The picture of the neck: dots show the notes; the root is highlighted; click a fret to hear it.

How: Click any dot or fret. Arrow keys move a cursor when the neck has focus.

When: This is the heart of the app – everything else changes what it shows.

29. Note labels

What: Show note names, interval numbers, both, or nothing on the dots.

How: Choose Labels in Settings (note, interval, both, none).

When: Interval labels are best for learning shapes; note names for learning the neck.

30. Show only some notes

What: Restricts the neck to all notes, guide tones (3rds and 7ths), triad tones, roots only, or everything but roots.

How: Choose from the Show menu above the neck.

When: Guide tones for soloing over changes; roots only for learning where the key notes are.

31. Practice patterns

What: Plays the scale in sequences: thirds, fourths, fifths, groups of three or four, 1-3-2-4 and more.

How: Choose a Practice pattern, then Play.

When: To build finger technique and hear the scale in new ways.

32. Strings filter

What: Shows only one string or a group of strings.

How: Choose from the Strings menu.

When: To practise melodies on one string, or to simplify the view.

33. Highlight a note

What: Draws extra attention to one note everywhere on the neck.

How: Choose a note in Highlight.

When: To learn where a particular note lives.

34. Piano and staff

What: Shows the same notes on a piano keyboard or on a music staff beside the neck.

How: Choose Also show: Piano keyboard or Staff notation, or open the Piano and staff section.

When: To connect the neck to the piano and to written music.

35. Position window

What: Limits the notes to a window of frets, so you see one hand position at a time.

How: Tick Position window and set the starting fret and width; or use the position buttons (Open, 5th, 7th…).

When: To learn a scale one box at a time.

36. Position buttons

What: One-click positions: All, 1–7 (scale positions) and Open, 5th, 7th, 9th, 12th (fret areas).

How: Click a number or a fret area.

When: To jump to the part of the neck you want.

37. Capo

What: Puts a capo on any fret from 1 to 12; chord names and sounds follow.

How: Choose Capo in the Fretboard section.

When: To match a song played with a capo, or to find easier shapes.

38. Number of frets

What: Show 12, 15, 17, 19, 22 or 24 frets.

How: Choose Frets.

When: Match your guitar, or look at only the lower neck.

39. Tuning

What: About 40 tunings: standard, drop D, DADGAD, open tunings and more – and the instrument tunings for bass, ukulele, mandolin, banjo, violin and others.

How: Choose Tuning. You can also nudge any string with ◀ ▶.

When: Whenever your instrument is not in standard tuning.

40. Instrument quick-switch

What: One-click buttons for Guitar, 7-string, Bass, Ukulele, Mandolin, Banjo and Baritone.

How: Click an instrument button.

When: To switch instrument without hunting through menus.

41. Strings & ranges

What: A table where you choose 1 to 32 strings, set each string’s tuning, and limit the first and last fret shown per string.

How: Click 🎸 Strings & ranges…

When: For 8-string guitars, basses, harps, or when you only want part of the neck.

42. Open-string sounds

What: Plays every open string in turn.

How: Click Play open strings.

When: To tune by ear or check the tuning.

43. Left-handed

What: Mirrors the neck for left-handed players.

How: Tick Left-handed in Settings.

When: If you play left-handed.

44. Fretless view

What: For violin, viola and cello: shows the neck without frets.

How: Tick Fretless view.

When: Bowed instruments with no frets.

45. Tab under the diagram

What: An ASCII tab of the current scale, chord or interval directly beneath the neck.

How: Tick Show tab under the diagram; Copy tab copies it.

When: To read or paste the fingering.

46. Neck Designer

What: Customises the neck: wood finish, inlays, marker shapes, sizes and labels.

How: Open the Neck Designer and choose from the menus.

When: To make the neck match your own guitar or your taste.

47. Piano and staff section

What: A full piano keyboard and a staff showing the scale or chord.

How: Open Piano and staff view.

When: For players who read music or play piano too.

48. Melodic patterns

What: Generates short phrases from the scale and shows them in tab.

How: Open Melodic patterns and click 🎼 New phrase.

When: When you want fresh licks to learn.

49. Copy the board as a picture

What: Copies the neck as an image for documents and chats.

How: Click 📋 Copy picture.

When: To put a diagram into a message or worksheet.

Chords, intervals, progressions

50. Chords mode

What: Shows every note of a chord on the neck, with 82 chord types.

How: Click the Chords tab, choose a root and a chord type, then ▶ Strum.

When: To see and hear chords and find voicings.

51. Chord voicings

What: Lists real playable shapes for the chord, with filters for string set, bass note and stretch.

How: Open Chord voicings, pick a shape, hear it with Play.

When: To choose a fingering that suits your hand.

52. Strum style

What: Down, up, alternate, all at once, or arpeggio – with adjustable speed.

How: Choose Strum and Strum speed in Settings.

When: To make chords sound like your playing.

53. Intervals mode

What: Shows an interval (or all intervals) from the root on every string.

How: Click the Intervals tab and pick an interval.

When: For ear training and learning interval shapes.

54. Progressions mode

What: Builds chord progressions in a key (I–IV–V, ii–V–I and many more), shows each chord and plays them with a backing.

How: Click Progressions, choose key type and progression, press Play progression.

When: To practise soloing over changes or learn common sequences.

55. Explore mode

What: You mark any notes you like on the neck; Play marked plays them in order.

How: Click Explore, click frets to mark them, then Play marked; Clear removes the marks.

When: To work out a riff, a lick or a chord shape of your own.

56. Quiz mode

What: Eleven kinds of test: find the note, name the note, find the interval, a 60-second challenge, and ear tests (interval, chord, scale, mode, progression, melodic dictation, absolute pitch).

How: Click Quiz, pick a type, answer on the neck or with the buttons.

When: To test yourself – with the strings you choose.

57. Progression library

What: 1,745 progressions in 18 categories (Andalusian, flamenco, blues, jazz and more) that play and export to MIDI, Guitar Pro and Power Tab.

How: Open the Progression library and pick a category.

When: When you want ready-made chord sequences.

Practice

58. Metronome

What: A click at your tempo with beats per bar, subdivisions and click sounds.

How: Open Practice and press ▶ Metronome; change the BPM slider and Beats.

When: Practise in time.

59. Tap tempo

What: Finds a tempo by tapping.

How: Tap the button in time with a song.

When: To copy the speed of a record.

60. Tempo presets and random tempo

What: One-click tempos (60–160) and a random tempo within a range.

How: Click a number, or 🎲 Random tempo.

When: To change speed quickly or vary your practice.

61. Drone

What: A sustained root, fifth, fourth, octave or major chord to play over.

How: Choose a Drone.

When: To hear how each note of a scale relates to the key.

62. Backing band

What: Drums (rock, ballad, funk, shuffle, bossa nova, reggae and 50+ more grooves) and bass notes with a mix control.

How: Choose Backing; tick Bass notes; adjust Mix.

When: To practise with a rhythm section.

63. Count-in

What: Counts a bar before playback begins.

How: Tick Count-in.

When: So you can start together with the click.

64. Humanize

What: Adds small random timing and strength changes so the backing does not sound robotic.

How: Move the Humanize slider.

When: For a natural feel.

65. Gap click

What: Plays some bars with the click and mutes the next ones.

How: Tick Gap click and set the bars to play and mute.

When: To test your inner sense of time.

66. Speed trainer

What: Raises the tempo a few BPM every few bars up to a maximum.

How: Tick Speed trainer and set the step, bars and maximum.

When: To build speed safely.

67. Daily challenge

What: A small new challenge every day.

How: Click 🎯 Daily challenge.

When: A reason to open the app each day.

68. Practice log

What: Records your minutes each day and your streak.

How: Click 📈 Practice log.

When: To track how much you practise.

69. Tuner

What: A microphone tuner that shows how sharp or flat each string is, plus a reference tone for each string.

How: Open Tuner, allow the microphone, play a string; or press 🔊 Reference tone.

When: To tune up. (This demo does not switch on your microphone.)

Reference sections

70. Information

What: Chords of the key (diatonic chords), the modes of the scale, the family table and the brightness ladder.

How: Open Information and click any chord or mode to jump to it.

When: To understand how a scale relates to chords and modes.

71. Circle of fifths

What: A circle showing key relationships and key signatures.

How: Open the Circle of fifths and click a key.

When: To find related keys and see sharps and flats.

72. Favourites

What: Your saved views, one click each.

How: Open Favourites; click ★ Save in any mode to add.

When: To keep your favourite setups close.

73. My notes

What: A free text box that is saved with the scale.

How: Open Information, type in My notes.

When: To write fingering reminders and ideas.

Settings & look

74. Settings

What: Every option in one place: tuning, labels, look, accessibility, sound, backing and profiles.

How: Open Settings and scroll – every option has a plain label.

When: Set it up once to suit you.

75. Solfège, Nashville and German names

What: Show Do-Re-Mi, Nashville numbers or German note names (H and B).

How: Tick Solfège, Nashville numbers or German note names.

When: Match the system you learned or your band uses.

76. Marker shapes and colours

What: Circle, square, diamond, triangle markers; colour-blind palette; accent colour; marker size.

How: Choose Markers; tick Colour-blind palette; pick an accent colour.

When: For clearer reading and personal taste.

77. Layout options

What: Compact layout, wide board with scrolling, Roman fret numbers, faint other notes, crosshair guides.

How: Tick the options you like.

When: To fit small screens or large projectors.

78. Text size and fonts

What: Larger text and a choice of fonts (Roboto, device font, serif, mono) and line spacing.

How: Use the Text size slider and Font menu.

When: For readability.

79. Accessibility

What: Reduced motion, large touch targets, strong focus rings, screen-reader announcements and speaking note names when tapped; keep the screen awake.

How: Tick what helps you.

When: For low vision, motor and screen-reader needs, or to keep the screen on while playing.

80. Sound

What: Choose the instrument sound (steel, nylon, electric, synth, organ, 12-string and more), sustain, reverb, tone, stereo spread and the concert pitch A4.

How: Open the Sound options in Settings.

When: To make the app sound like your instrument, or match a piano tuned to 442 Hz.

81. Scale tempo

What: How fast the scale plays; or follow the metronome tempo.

How: Use the Scale tempo slider, or tick “follows the metronome”.

When: To listen slowly while learning.

82. MIDI keyboard

What: Play notes on a MIDI keyboard to light them up on the neck (where the browser supports it).

How: Tick MIDI keyboard input and allow access.

When: To see what you play on a keyboard.

83. Settings profiles

What: Save all your settings under a name and load them later.

How: Type a name and click Save profile; choose it and click Load.

When: To switch between “practice” and “teaching” setups.

Share & export

84. Export picture, sheet and MIDI

What: PNG and SVG pictures of the neck, a printable sheet, and a MIDI file of the scale or chord.

How: Open Share, export & backup and click Export PNG / SVG / MIDI or Print sheet.

When: To make handouts and import into a DAW. (The demo does not download files.)

85. Export Studio

What: A full export workspace for study sheets, diagrams and tab in many formats.

How: Click Export Studio…

When: When you need polished printed material.

86. Copy link and settings code

What: Copies a link to the current view, or a text code of all your settings that another device can load.

How: Click Copy link, or Copy settings code and paste on the other device with Load code.

When: To share a setup or move to a new device.

87. Backup and restore

What: Downloads all your settings and library as a file, and loads it back.

How: Click Download backup; later Load backup file.

When: Before you clear the browser, change device or reset.

88. Export favourites, log and notes

What: Favourites as JSON, practice log as CSV, notes as text, or the current view as Markdown.

How: Use the buttons under Share, export & backup.

When: To keep your data in other tools.

89. Get the app

What: Install FretLab on Windows, Mac, Linux, Android, iPhone and iPad, or save the one-file version.

How: Click ⬇ or Get the app…

When: To use FretLab like any other app, offline.

90. Reset

What: Resets sections or everything to factory settings – with a confirmation.

How: Use Reset everything or the section reset buttons.

When: If settings get tangled. Make a backup first.

Learn & play

91. Lessons

What: 2,223 interactive lessons in 41 units, from the very basics to advanced, in a clean separate view with examples that play on the neck.

How: Click 🎓 Lessons; use ← → for the previous and next lesson and Space to play an example.

When: When you want a guided path instead of exploring.

92. Games

What: 100 games for notes, intervals, chords, scales, modes, keys and ear training.

How: Click 🎮 Games, pick one, answer with the mouse or keys 1–4.

When: To make practice fun.

93. Video studio

What: Turns the current scale, chord, interval, progression or song into an animated video with sound (MP4, FLV or GIF; 16:9, square or vertical).

How: Click 🎬 Video, choose the format and press record/export.

When: To post a lesson or share an idea.

94. My library

What: Your own scales, chords, progressions, strumming patterns and drum grooves – they appear in the menus.

How: Click 📚 My library and add items.

When: When the app’s lists do not have what you play.

95. Guide

What: The complete how-to PDF with screenshots and install instructions.

How: Click 📖 Guide.

When: When you want a full reference. (The demo does not open the file.)

190. Finger drill

What: Generates 8 measures of random 2-, 3- or 4-note finger patterns from fingers 1–4 in any order (1-3-2-4, 1-3, 2-1-3 …), the same on every string or different on each, with the neck, the tab and sound.

How: Click 🖐 Drill, choose the notes per pattern, the strings, the starting fret and the tempo, press ↻ New drill, then ▶ Play all.

When: When you want to build finger independence and a clean left hand.
