# ManicRulesGuitar – feature reference

200 features, each with what it is, how to use it and when to use it. The same text is shown by the guided demo (Shift+D).

## Getting around

### 1. Welcome to ManicRulesGuitar
- **What it is:** ManicRulesGuitar shows any scale, chord or interval on a fretboard, plays it, and teaches it – on guitar and 12 other instruments, fully offline.
- **How to use it:** Use Next / Back (or the ← → keys). Space pauses and resumes auto-play. Press Esc to leave: your own settings are put back exactly as they were.
- **When to use it:** Any time you want to see what the app can do, or want a refresher after an update.
- **Shortcut:** Shift+D starts this demo

### 2. The header menu
- **What it is:** The Menu button sits at the top right of the header. It opens the bar with the mode tabs and the big buttons (Lessons, Games, Video, Library, Guide); closed, the header is one slim line with only the ManicRulesGuitar name and the Menu button.
- **How to use it:** Click ▾ Menu at the top right (or press H) to open it, ▴ Hide menu to close it. Your choice is remembered. On a phone the Mobile / Desktop switch is in the opened menu and also floats at the bottom right.
- **When to use it:** Open it when you want to switch mode or open a full-screen area; close it when you just want to practise.
- **Shortcut:** H

### 3. Mode tabs
- **What it is:** Six ways to look at the neck: Scales, Chords, Intervals, Progressions, Explore (mark your own notes) and Quiz.
- **How to use it:** Click a tab, or press 1–6.
- **When to use it:** Scales for soloing, Chords for rhythm playing, Intervals for ear and theory, Progressions for songs, Explore for free marking, Quiz to test yourself.
- **Shortcut:** 1–6

### 4. Navigate menu
- **What it is:** A jump menu listing every section and library page. It always opens fully on screen, on desktop and phone.
- **How to use it:** Click ☰ Navigate (or press G) and pick a section; it opens and scrolls into view.
- **When to use it:** When the page is long and you want a section fast.
- **Shortcut:** G

### 5. Simple view / Show all sections
- **What it is:** The page starts with only the fretboard shown. Every other section is hidden until you ask for it, and each section can be collapsed or expanded; your choices are remembered.
- **How to use it:** Click Show all sections to reveal everything, or ⊞ / ⊟ (or O) to expand or collapse all cards. Click a section title to open or close it.
- **When to use it:** Use the simple view for practising, the full view when you explore the tools.
- **Shortcut:** O

### 6. Tools menu
- **What it is:** A panel of extra actions with a button for every section and library page of the app. It also holds the Show the tip note checkbox, which is off by default.
- **How to use it:** Click ⋯ Tools and choose an action or a section; tick Show the tip note to bring back the note under the header.
- **When to use it:** When the header gets crowded, especially on a phone, or to jump to any part of the app.

### 7. Search sections and controls
- **What it is:** A search box under the header that finds any section, library page or control by name.
- **How to use it:** Click the box (or press /), type a few letters and pick a result; the section opens and the control flashes.
- **When to use it:** When you know what you want but not where it is.
- **Shortcut:** /

### 8. Mobile view
- **What it is:** A phone layout: the fretboard on top, a dock of icons below that pins sections, with Tools, Controls, Open pos., Jam Band and Export in the dock.
- **How to use it:** Tap the 📱 Mobile / 🖥 Desktop button (it floats at the bottom right on phones). Tap once; a second tap within half a second is ignored so it cannot get stuck.
- **When to use it:** On a phone or small tablet.

### 9. Quick search
- **What it is:** Type any scale, chord or section name and jump straight to it.
- **How to use it:** Click 🔎, type a few letters, press Enter.
- **When to use it:** When you know the name of the scale or chord you want.

### 10. Undo and redo
- **What it is:** Steps backwards and forwards through your recent changes to key, scale, chord and settings.
- **How to use it:** Click ↶ or ↷.
- **When to use it:** After changing something by mistake.

### 11. Theme
- **What it is:** Switches between light and dark. Seven more themes (sepia, midnight, forest, rose, stage red, high contrast and automatic) are in Settings.
- **How to use it:** Click 🌙 (Shift+T cycles every theme).
- **When to use it:** Dark for stages and evenings, light for daylight, high contrast for low vision.
- **Shortcut:** Shift+T

### 12. Help and shortcuts
- **What it is:** A short list of every keyboard shortcut, with a search box.
- **How to use it:** Click ? or press ?.
- **When to use it:** When you want to learn the quick keys.
- **Shortcut:** ?

### 13. Focus mode
- **What it is:** Hides everything except the neck.
- **How to use it:** Press F (Esc leaves).
- **When to use it:** For recording, teaching or looking at the neck on a small screen.
- **Shortcut:** F

### 14. Presentation mode
- **What it is:** A big, clean view for projectors and lessons.
- **How to use it:** Click 🖥 or press Shift+P.
- **When to use it:** When you are showing the neck to a class or audience.
- **Shortcut:** Shift+P

### 15. Mute and volume
- **What it is:** Mute everything with one button; the 🔊 slider sets the master volume.
- **How to use it:** Click 🔈, or drag the slider.
- **When to use it:** To practise silently or to balance with a backing track.
- **Shortcut:** Shift+M

### 16. Tip of the day
- **What it is:** A small hint at the top. Next tip shows another; ✕ hides them for good.
- **How to use it:** Click Next tip.
- **When to use it:** While you are learning your way round.

## Scales & controls

### 17. Choose a scale
- **What it is:** 182 scales in families – major and its modes, minor types, pentatonics, blues, jazz, world and exotic scales.
- **How to use it:** Open the Scale menu and choose, or type in the search box. The neck updates at once.
- **When to use it:** Pick the scale that matches the key or mood of the music you are playing.

### 18. Search for a scale
- **What it is:** A search box that filters the scale list as you type.
- **How to use it:** Type part of a name, for example “harm”, then press Enter.
- **When to use it:** When you do not know which family a scale lives in.

### 19. Root note
- **What it is:** Twelve buttons choose the key (the root note).
- **How to use it:** Click a note, or use ← → to move the root one semitone (hold Shift for a fifth).
- **When to use it:** To play the same scale in another key.
- **Shortcut:** ← →

### 20. Spelling
- **What it is:** Chooses sharps, flats or automatic correct spelling for the key.
- **How to use it:** Click the Spelling button to cycle: Auto, Sharps ♯, Flats ♭.
- **When to use it:** Auto gives you proper note names (for example F♯ in G major, B♭ in F major).

### 21. Play the scale
- **What it is:** Plays the whole scale up and down with the sound you chose in Settings.
- **How to use it:** Click ▶ Play scale or press Space. ■ stops it.
- **When to use it:** To hear a scale before you learn it, or to practise along.
- **Shortcut:** Space

### 22. Direction
- **What it is:** Up, down, up and down, or random order when the scale plays.
- **How to use it:** Choose a Direction from the menu.
- **When to use it:** Use random for ear training.

### 23. Loop
- **What it is:** Repeats the scale again and again.
- **How to use it:** Tick Loop and press Play; press Stop to end.
- **When to use it:** To play along as long as you like.

### 24. End on root chord
- **What it is:** After the scale, plays the main chord that belongs to it, so the ear hears where the scale “lands”.
- **How to use it:** Tick End on root chord, then press Play.
- **When to use it:** To hear the tonic chord and tell a mode’s mood.

### 25. Random
- **What it is:** Picks a random root and scale (or chord) to practise.
- **How to use it:** Click 🎲.
- **When to use it:** For warm-ups and surprise practice.

### 26. Save a favourite
- **What it is:** Stores the current view (mode, key, scale, chord, tuning) in your Favourites.
- **How to use it:** Click ★ Save; open it again from the Favourites section.
- **When to use it:** To return to a setup you use often.

### 27. Scale library
- **What it is:** A browsable library of all scales with note spellings and formulas.
- **How to use it:** Click 📚 Library.
- **When to use it:** To compare scales and read their note names.

### 28. Compare two scales
- **What it is:** Shows a second scale on the same root on top of the first, so you see what is different.
- **How to use it:** Choose a scale in the Compare menu.
- **When to use it:** To understand how Dorian differs from natural minor, for example.

### 29. Overlay a chord
- **What it is:** Draws a chord of the current key on top of the scale.
- **How to use it:** Choose a chord in the Overlay menu.
- **When to use it:** To see which scale notes make up the chord you are playing over.

## The fretboard

### 30. The fretboard
- **What it is:** The picture of the neck: dots show the notes; the root is highlighted; click a fret to hear it.
- **How to use it:** Click any dot or fret. Arrow keys move a cursor when the neck has focus.
- **When to use it:** This is the heart of the app – everything else changes what it shows.

### 31. Note labels
- **What it is:** Show note names, interval numbers, both, or nothing on the dots.
- **How to use it:** Choose Labels in Settings (note, interval, both, none).
- **When to use it:** Interval labels are best for learning shapes; note names for learning the neck.

### 32. Show only some notes
- **What it is:** Restricts the neck to all notes, guide tones (3rds and 7ths), triad tones, roots only, or everything but roots.
- **How to use it:** Choose from the Show menu above the neck.
- **When to use it:** Guide tones for soloing over changes; roots only for learning where the key notes are.

### 33. Practice patterns
- **What it is:** Plays the scale in sequences: thirds, fourths, fifths, groups of three or four, 1-3-2-4 and more.
- **How to use it:** Choose a Practice pattern, then Play.
- **When to use it:** To build finger technique and hear the scale in new ways.

### 34. Strings filter
- **What it is:** Shows only one string or a group of strings.
- **How to use it:** Choose from the Strings menu.
- **When to use it:** To practise melodies on one string, or to simplify the view.

### 35. Highlight a note
- **What it is:** Draws extra attention to one note everywhere on the neck.
- **How to use it:** Choose a note in Highlight.
- **When to use it:** To learn where a particular note lives.

### 36. Piano and staff
- **What it is:** Shows the same notes on a piano keyboard or on a music staff beside the neck.
- **How to use it:** Choose Also show: Piano keyboard or Staff notation, or open the Piano and staff section.
- **When to use it:** To connect the neck to the piano and to written music.

### 37. Position window
- **What it is:** Limits the notes to a window of frets, so you see one hand position at a time.
- **How to use it:** Tick Position window and set the starting fret and width; or use the position buttons (Open, 5th, 7th…).
- **When to use it:** To learn a scale one box at a time.

### 38. Position buttons
- **What it is:** One-click positions: All, 1–7 (scale positions) and Open, 5th, 7th, 9th, 12th (fret areas).
- **How to use it:** Click a number or a fret area.
- **When to use it:** To jump to the part of the neck you want.

### 39. Capo
- **What it is:** Puts a capo on any fret from 1 to 12; chord names and sounds follow.
- **How to use it:** Choose Capo in the Fretboard section.
- **When to use it:** To match a song played with a capo, or to find easier shapes.

### 40. Number of frets
- **What it is:** Show 12, 15, 17, 19, 22 or 24 frets.
- **How to use it:** Choose Frets.
- **When to use it:** Match your guitar, or look at only the lower neck.

### 41. Tuning
- **What it is:** About 40 tunings: standard, drop D, DADGAD, open tunings and more – and the instrument tunings for bass, ukulele, mandolin, banjo, violin and others.
- **How to use it:** Choose Tuning. You can also nudge any string with ◀ ▶.
- **When to use it:** Whenever your instrument is not in standard tuning.

### 42. Fretboard option dropdowns
- **What it is:** All fretboard options live in dropdown buttons above the neck: Instrument, Root, Scale, Play, Strings, Show, Diagram, Position, Fret window, Practice pattern, Tune and Export.
- **How to use it:** Click a button to open its options, click it again to close; ⊞ All options shows every option at once. The Controls button in the header opens the Root dropdown.
- **When to use it:** To keep the neck large and still reach every setting.

### 43. Searchable dropdowns
- **What it is:** Every dropdown list in the app opens as a searchable list, like the demo song picker: type to narrow long lists such as the 182 scales, and click an option to choose it.
- **How to use it:** Click any dropdown. Type to search (lists of more than eight options), use ↑ ↓ and Enter, or click an option; Esc or ✕ closes it.
- **When to use it:** To find an item in a long list quickly, on a keyboard or a phone.

### 44. Open position (frets 0–5)
- **What it is:** A button that limits the neck to the open strings and frets 0 to 5, for any tuning.
- **How to use it:** Click Open pos. above the neck (or in the mobile dock); click again for all frets.
- **When to use it:** To learn first-position scales and chords.

### 45. Fretboard Export dropdown
- **What it is:** One dropdown with every export for the fretboard: pictures (PNG, SVG, copy, print), MIDI and tab, audio and video, Export Studio, Export Hub, settings, link, fullscreen and share.
- **How to use it:** Open Export beside Tune and click an action.
- **When to use it:** When you want to take what you see out of the app.

### 46. Octave-accurate animation
- **What it is:** When a scale, interval or chord plays, the neck lights only the octave that is actually sounding.
- **How to use it:** Press Play on any scale, chord or interval and watch the lit notes.
- **When to use it:** To see exactly which fret each sounding note comes from.

### 47. Instrument quick-switch
- **What it is:** One-click buttons for Guitar, 7-string, Bass, Ukulele, Mandolin, Banjo and Baritone.
- **How to use it:** Click an instrument button.
- **When to use it:** To switch instrument without hunting through menus.

### 48. Strings & ranges
- **What it is:** A table where you choose 1 to 32 strings, set each string’s tuning, and limit the first and last fret shown per string.
- **How to use it:** Click 🎸 Strings & ranges…
- **When to use it:** For 8-string guitars, basses, harps, or when you only want part of the neck.

### 49. Open-string sounds
- **What it is:** Plays every open string in turn.
- **How to use it:** Click Play open strings.
- **When to use it:** To tune by ear or check the tuning.

### 50. Left-handed
- **What it is:** Mirrors the neck for left-handed players.
- **How to use it:** Tick Left-handed in Settings.
- **When to use it:** If you play left-handed.

### 51. Fretless view
- **What it is:** For violin, viola and cello: shows the neck without frets.
- **How to use it:** Tick Fretless view.
- **When to use it:** Bowed instruments with no frets.

### 52. Tab under the diagram
- **What it is:** An ASCII tab of the current scale, chord or interval directly beneath the neck.
- **How to use it:** Tick Show tab under the diagram; Copy tab copies it.
- **When to use it:** To read or paste the fingering.

### 53. Neck Designer
- **What it is:** Customises the neck: wood finish, inlays, marker shapes, sizes and labels.
- **How to use it:** Open the Neck Designer and choose from the menus.
- **When to use it:** To make the neck match your own guitar or your taste.

### 54. Piano and staff section
- **What it is:** A full piano keyboard and a staff showing the scale or chord.
- **How to use it:** Open Piano and staff view.
- **When to use it:** For players who read music or play piano too.

### 55. Melodic patterns
- **What it is:** Generates short phrases from the scale and shows them in tab.
- **How to use it:** Open Melodic patterns and click 🎼 New phrase.
- **When to use it:** When you want fresh licks to learn.

### 56. Copy the board as a picture
- **What it is:** Copies the neck as an image for documents and chats.
- **How to use it:** Click 📋 Copy picture.
- **When to use it:** To put a diagram into a message or worksheet.

## Chords, intervals, progressions

### 57. Chords mode
- **What it is:** Shows every note of a chord on the neck, with 82 chord types.
- **How to use it:** Click the Chords tab, choose a root and a chord type, then ▶ Strum.
- **When to use it:** To see and hear chords and find voicings.

### 58. Chord voicings
- **What it is:** Lists real playable shapes for the chord, with filters for string set, bass note and stretch.
- **How to use it:** Open Chord voicings, pick a shape, hear it with Play.
- **When to use it:** To choose a fingering that suits your hand.

### 59. Strum style
- **What it is:** Down, up, alternate, all at once, or arpeggio – with adjustable speed.
- **How to use it:** Choose Strum and Strum speed in Settings.
- **When to use it:** To make chords sound like your playing.

### 60. Intervals mode
- **What it is:** Shows an interval (or all intervals) from the root on every string.
- **How to use it:** Click the Intervals tab and pick an interval.
- **When to use it:** For ear training and learning interval shapes.

### 61. Progressions mode
- **What it is:** Builds chord progressions in a key (I–IV–V, ii–V–I and many more), shows each chord and plays them with a backing.
- **How to use it:** Click Progressions, choose key type and progression, press Play progression.
- **When to use it:** To practise soloing over changes or learn common sequences.

### 62. Explore mode
- **What it is:** You mark any notes you like on the neck; Play marked plays them in order.
- **How to use it:** Click Explore, click frets to mark them, then Play marked; Clear removes the marks.
- **When to use it:** To work out a riff, a lick or a chord shape of your own.

### 63. Quiz mode
- **What it is:** Eleven kinds of test: find the note, name the note, find the interval, a 60-second challenge, and ear tests (interval, chord, scale, mode, progression, melodic dictation, absolute pitch).
- **How to use it:** Click Quiz, pick a type, answer on the neck or with the buttons.
- **When to use it:** To test yourself – with the strings you choose.

### 64. Progression library
- **What it is:** 1,745 progressions in 18 categories (Andalusian, flamenco, blues, jazz and more) that play and export to MIDI, Guitar Pro and Power Tab.
- **How to use it:** Open the Progression library and pick a category.
- **When to use it:** When you want ready-made chord sequences.

## Practice

### 65. Metronome
- **What it is:** A click at your tempo with beats per bar, subdivisions and click sounds.
- **How to use it:** Open Practice and press ▶ Metronome; change the BPM slider and Beats.
- **When to use it:** Practise in time.

### 66. Tap tempo
- **What it is:** Finds a tempo by tapping.
- **How to use it:** Tap the button in time with a song.
- **When to use it:** To copy the speed of a record.

### 67. Tempo presets and random tempo
- **What it is:** One-click tempos (60–160) and a random tempo within a range.
- **How to use it:** Click a number, or 🎲 Random tempo.
- **When to use it:** To change speed quickly or vary your practice.

### 68. Drone
- **What it is:** A sustained root, fifth, fourth, octave or major chord to play over.
- **How to use it:** Choose a Drone.
- **When to use it:** To hear how each note of a scale relates to the key.

### 69. Backing band
- **What it is:** Drums (rock, ballad, funk, shuffle, bossa nova, reggae and 50+ more grooves) and bass notes with a mix control.
- **How to use it:** Choose Backing; tick Bass notes; adjust Mix.
- **When to use it:** To practise with a rhythm section.

### 70. Jam Band demo picker
- **What it is:** A searchable list of all demo songs, with a style filter, previous, next and random buttons, and a Nylon guitar only checkbox for a plain nylon-guitar band.
- **How to use it:** Open Jam Band, click the demo button, type to search (name, style, tempo or chords) and click a song. Tick Nylon guitar only to drop bass, drums, keys and lead.
- **When to use it:** To find a backing track quickly, or to practise with just a guitar.

### 71. Count-in
- **What it is:** Counts a bar before playback begins.
- **How to use it:** Tick Count-in.
- **When to use it:** So you can start together with the click.

### 72. Humanize
- **What it is:** Adds small random timing and strength changes so the backing does not sound robotic.
- **How to use it:** Move the Humanize slider.
- **When to use it:** For a natural feel.

### 73. Gap click
- **What it is:** Plays some bars with the click and mutes the next ones.
- **How to use it:** Tick Gap click and set the bars to play and mute.
- **When to use it:** To test your inner sense of time.

### 74. Speed trainer
- **What it is:** Raises the tempo a few BPM every few bars up to a maximum.
- **How to use it:** Tick Speed trainer and set the step, bars and maximum.
- **When to use it:** To build speed safely.

### 75. Daily challenge
- **What it is:** A small new challenge every day.
- **How to use it:** Click 🎯 Daily challenge.
- **When to use it:** A reason to open the app each day.

### 76. Practice log
- **What it is:** Records your minutes each day and your streak.
- **How to use it:** Click 📈 Practice log.
- **When to use it:** To track how much you practise.

### 77. Tuner
- **What it is:** A microphone tuner that shows how sharp or flat each string is, plus a reference tone for each string.
- **How to use it:** Open Tuner, allow the microphone, play a string; or press 🔊 Reference tone.
- **When to use it:** To tune up. (This demo does not switch on your microphone.)

## Reference sections

### 78. Information
- **What it is:** Chords of the key (diatonic chords), the modes of the scale, the family table and the brightness ladder.
- **How to use it:** Open Information and click any chord or mode to jump to it.
- **When to use it:** To understand how a scale relates to chords and modes.

### 79. Circle of fifths
- **What it is:** A circle showing key relationships and key signatures.
- **How to use it:** Open the Circle of fifths and click a key.
- **When to use it:** To find related keys and see sharps and flats.

### 80. Favourites
- **What it is:** Your saved views, one click each.
- **How to use it:** Open Favourites; click ★ Save in any mode to add.
- **When to use it:** To keep your favourite setups close.

### 81. My notes
- **What it is:** A free text box that is saved with the scale.
- **How to use it:** Open Information, type in My notes.
- **When to use it:** To write fingering reminders and ideas.

## Settings & look

### 82. Settings
- **What it is:** Every option in one place: tuning, labels, look, accessibility, sound, backing and profiles.
- **How to use it:** Open Settings and scroll – every option has a plain label.
- **When to use it:** Set it up once to suit you.

### 83. Solfège, Nashville and German names
- **What it is:** Show Do-Re-Mi, Nashville numbers or German note names (H and B).
- **How to use it:** Tick Solfège, Nashville numbers or German note names.
- **When to use it:** Match the system you learned or your band uses.

### 84. Marker shapes and colours
- **What it is:** Circle, square, diamond, triangle markers; colour-blind palette; accent colour; marker size.
- **How to use it:** Choose Markers; tick Colour-blind palette; pick an accent colour.
- **When to use it:** For clearer reading and personal taste.

### 85. Layout options
- **What it is:** Compact layout, wide board with scrolling, Roman fret numbers, faint other notes, crosshair guides.
- **How to use it:** Tick the options you like.
- **When to use it:** To fit small screens or large projectors.

### 86. Text size and fonts
- **What it is:** Larger text and a choice of fonts (Roboto, device font, serif, mono) and line spacing.
- **How to use it:** Use the Text size slider and Font menu.
- **When to use it:** For readability.

### 87. Accessibility
- **What it is:** Reduced motion, large touch targets, strong focus rings, screen-reader announcements and speaking note names when tapped; keep the screen awake.
- **How to use it:** Tick what helps you.
- **When to use it:** For low vision, motor and screen-reader needs, or to keep the screen on while playing.

### 88. Sound
- **What it is:** Choose the instrument sound (steel, nylon, electric, synth, organ, 12-string and more), sustain, reverb, tone, stereo spread and the concert pitch A4.
- **How to use it:** Open the Sound options in Settings.
- **When to use it:** To make the app sound like your instrument, or match a piano tuned to 442 Hz.

### 89. Scale tempo
- **What it is:** How fast the scale plays; or follow the metronome tempo.
- **How to use it:** Use the Scale tempo slider, or tick “follows the metronome”.
- **When to use it:** To listen slowly while learning.

### 90. MIDI keyboard
- **What it is:** Play notes on a MIDI keyboard to light them up on the neck (where the browser supports it).
- **How to use it:** Tick MIDI keyboard input and allow access.
- **When to use it:** To see what you play on a keyboard.

### 91. Settings profiles
- **What it is:** Save all your settings under a name and load them later.
- **How to use it:** Type a name and click Save profile; choose it and click Load.
- **When to use it:** To switch between “practice” and “teaching” setups.

## Share & export

### 92. Export picture, sheet and MIDI
- **What it is:** PNG and SVG pictures of the neck, a printable sheet, and a MIDI file of the scale or chord. Every fretboard export is also in the Export dropdown above the neck.
- **How to use it:** Open the Export dropdown beside Tune (or Share, export & backup) and click PNG, SVG, MIDI file or Print sheet.
- **When to use it:** To make handouts and import into a DAW. (The demo does not download files.)

### 93. Export Studio
- **What it is:** A full export workspace for study sheets, diagrams, tab and Jam Band material in many formats. The Selection tab sets how many diagrams per page and their size; the Page tab sets the paper size and orientation; the Jam Band & songs tab exports demo songs, patterns and progressions.
- **How to use it:** Click Export Studio…, then open the Selection, Page or Jam Band & songs tab.
- **When to use it:** When you need polished printed material or a whole set of files.

### 94. Copy link and settings code
- **What it is:** Copies a link to the current view, or a text code of all your settings that another device can load.
- **How to use it:** Click Copy link, or Copy settings code and paste on the other device with Load code.
- **When to use it:** To share a setup or move to a new device.

### 95. Export all demos and patterns
- **What it is:** Exports every demo song, strum, drum groove, arpeggio, progression, scale, chord and interval as MIDI, Guitar Pro, Power Tab, ASCII, WAV, MP3, MP4 or FLV. You can add drums to every demo, change the groove, and edit tempo, time signature, chord length, repeats, key, track on/off and every instrument for the whole batch.
- **How to use it:** Click Export all (Tools or the Jam Band), tick formats and what to export, set the drum part and Edit every exported item, then Export.
- **When to use it:** To build a complete practice library or a set of backing tracks in one go.

### 96. Video and audio from a scale, chord or interval
- **What it is:** The Jam Band audio and video export can use the scale, chord or interval on the fretboard as its source, not only demo songs.
